A stereo effects unit for the TrimUI Brick · KNULLI · v0.2

Plug in.
Play.
Echo out.

BrickFX turns the Brick into a stereo effects box. Plug a USB synth or a USB audio interface into the Brick, and any sound can go through it: synths, drum machines, a guitar, a mic. It comes back out of the jack through four effects in a chain: drive, modulation, delay and reverb.

How it works The chain

v0.2 runs on the Brick · In development · Same look and controls as BrickSampler

SETUP

Two ways in, one way out. Use a USB synth on its own, or a USB audio interface for everything else.

  1. 01Connect your sound to the Brick's USB port. A synth with USB audio, like the FM-1, plugs in directly. For anything else, use a USB audio interface: whatever you can plug into it can go through BrickFX.
  2. 02Run a cable from the Brick's headphone jack into your audio interface or mixer.
  3. 03Open BrickFX, pick the input and the channels on the I/O page, and play.

The FM-1 and an M-Audio interface both show up on the Brick as USB audio. Most class-compliant interfaces (the ones that need no driver) should work the same way.

Pages: I/O · CHAIN · DRV · MOD · DLY · REV · SET. If your synth sounds quiet over USB, turn up Input gain on the I/O page.

THE CHAIN

Four sections, one after the other: DRIVE, MOD, DELAY, REVERB. In each one you pick one type, or switch it off. Every section has its own page, with a picture of what it does: the echoes, the drive curve, the modulation wave, the size of the room.

DRIVE

Drive, fuzz or crush. From a warm push to broken and dirty.

MOD

Chorus, phaser, tremolo, auto-pan or vibrato.

DELAY

Digital, tape or analog. Stereo, ping-pong or mono, time in ms or locked to a note value, tap tempo, high and low cut in the repeats, width.

REVERB

Room, plate or hall, in stereo.

PRESETS

Ready-made sounds to start from, one button away.

MIDI

MIDI learn for any value, MIDI clock for the delay, program change for the presets.

With all four sections on, BrickFX uses less than 10% of the Brick's CPU. There is room for more.

UNDER THE HOOD

The audio runs in C on its own real-time thread, like the engine in BrickSampler. It reads the USB input directly and writes straight to the jack, so the screen can never hold up the sound.

Your USB device and the Brick each have their own clock, and over a few minutes they slowly drift apart: the FM-1 runs about 0.2% faster than the Brick. A small adaptive resampler keeps them in step, so there are no clicks or drop-outs on long sessions.

The goal is 10 to 15 ms from input to output.
